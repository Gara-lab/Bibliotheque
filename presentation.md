Understood. I would keep the scope narrow: we're not trying to find the biggest market for presentation templates. We're trying to identify buyers who already have a recurring, expensive problem that a ready-made presentation can solve without requiring your involvement.

That distinction is important.

I would evaluate potential ICPs against four criteria:

1. Existing demand: They already spend money or time producing training, educational or business presentations.
2. Clear pain: Creating these materials is a recurring burden, not an occasional inconvenience.
3. Product fit: A prebuilt, professionally structured deck can solve most of the problem without customization.
4. Buying simplicity: The person experiencing the problem can purchase the product directly, without lengthy procurement or sales discussions.

I would also distinguish between two markets:

- End users: People who need presentations to do their jobs.
- Presentation producers: People whose business involves repeatedly creating presentations for other people.

The second group could be particularly interesting because their need is potentially recurring.

## 4. My recommendation

I would start with independent corporate trainers and training consultants, with coaches and course creators as the second audience.

Here's why:

The ideal customer is not necessarily someone who needs a presentation.

It's someone whose business depends on delivering information to other people, repeatedly, and who can reuse a professionally prepared presentation to make money or save production costs.

That gives us a much stronger value proposition:

> Stop spending hours building training presentations from scratch. Get professionally structured, ready-to-use training decks for your next workshop or client.

It also lends itself naturally to your two product levels:

- Standardized packages: Ready-to-use decks for common training needs, sold individually.
- Premium decks: More comprehensive, specialized training systems, potentially including multiple presentations and supporting materials.

The second category could command higher prices without necessarily requiring custom work.

## 5. What I want to investigate next

Before settling on this ICP, I think we need to validate one important question:

Are independent trainers actually buying ready-made training materials, or are they predominantly creating their own and purchasing custom content?

That distinction could change our entire target audience.

I'd investigate:

- Existing marketplaces selling complete training decks, not just slide designs.
- Actual product prices and evidence of purchases/reviews.
- Discussions among independent trainers about preparing training materials.
- Specific training subjects for which buyers already pay for ready-made resources.
- Whether buyers prefer individual presentations or complete training kits.

I don't want to confuse a large industry with a proven market for your particular product.

My current hypothesis: independent trainers and training consultants are the best starting ICP, but we should validate their purchasing behaviour before committing.
